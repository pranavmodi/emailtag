Classification Rules for Accounts Receivable:

1. Subject Line Keywords:
 a. If the subject contains any strong financial/billing keywords such as:
  – “Bill”, “Billing”, “Lien”, “Payment”, “Balance”, “W‑9”, “Offer”, “Remittance”, “Payment Date”, “Billing Report Request”, or any phrase indicating a billing process,
  then classify the email as Accounts Receivable.
 b. Even if imaging-related words like “IMG” or “Medical Records” appear, if any of the strong financial indicators above are present, default to Accounts Receivable.
 c. For fax notification subjects such as “EXTERNAL: A fax has arrived from: …”, check for additional context; if no distinct imaging portal or viewing instructions are included, classify as Accounts Receivable.

2. Content Patterns and Phrases:
 a. IF the email body includes phrases such as “bill balance”, “payment date”, “lien signed”, “process payment”, “generate bill”, “remittance”, “financial reconciliation”, “W‑9”, “negotiation offer”, or explicit instructions linked to payment processing, classify as Accounts Receivable.
 b. If the email mixes imaging details with any billing or lien processing information (e.g., “Medical Records & Billing Report Request”), determine the primary intent by checking for explicit financial instructions. If financial instructions are prominent, classify as Accounts Receivable.

3. Associated Tags & Metadata:
 a. Emails tagged with billing or financial labels (e.g., “Bill or Balance Request”, “Bill Offer”, “Rad Lien”, “Tech Lien”, “ASL”, “SLA Applies”) are immediately classified as Accounts Receivable.
 b. If any associated tag implies financial processing despite the presence of imaging keywords, classify under Accounts Receivable.

4. Purpose and Intent:
 a. Classify as Accounts Receivable when the main purpose of the email is to discuss or initiate payment, provide billing information, set up payment arrangements, or process financial documents (including liens and billing forms).
 b. For emails with mixed content, require that any explicit billing instructions (e.g., “contact billing”, “process payment”, “financial coordinator”) drive the classification to Accounts Receivable.

5. Unique Identifiers and Special Cases:
 a. Fax notifications (e.g., “You received a fax! Your fax is attached.”) should normally be routed to Accounts Receivable if there is no explicit imaging portal or download instructions provided.
 b. Any email referencing a financial contact (e.g., “PI Billing Coordinator”, “Cedar Financial”, or similar) should be classified as Accounts Receivable regardless of any incidental imaging references.
