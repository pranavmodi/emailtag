=== Scheduling RULES ===

Classification Rules for Scheduling:

1. **Subject Line Keywords**: 
   - If the subject line contains keywords such as "Referral", "Scheduled", "Reschedule", "Appointment", "MRI", "Scan", or "DOL" (Date of Loss), it is likely a Scheduling email.

2. **Content Patterns and Phrases**:
   - If the content includes phrases like "patient has been scheduled", "reschedule an MRI", "appointment times", "scheduling department", "call our scheduling dept.", or "referral has been entered into our scheduling system", classify it as Scheduling.
   - Mentions of specific actions related to scheduling, such as "contact us to reschedule or cancel", "reach out to the patient", or "call our direct scheduling line", indicate a Scheduling email.

3. **Associated Tags**:
   - Emails tagged with "Order Entered", "Order Pending", or "RX" are often associated with Scheduling.

4. **Purpose or Intent**:
   - If the main purpose of the email is to confirm, arrange, or modify an appointment or referral, it belongs to the Scheduling category.
   - Emails that discuss the logistics of patient appointments, such as providing directions, rescheduling, or confirming attendance, are typically Scheduling emails.

5. **Unique Characteristics**:
   - Emails that provide patient IDs and request the use of these IDs for future communications are indicative of Scheduling.
   - References to patient portals for viewing appointment times, answering prescreening questions, or signing liens are unique to Scheduling emails.
   - If the email includes instructions for patients to contact a specific phone number for scheduling-related inquiries, it is likely a Scheduling email.

By applying these rules, you can effectively classify emails into the Scheduling category based on their content, intent, and associated metadata.

=== Accounts Receivable RULES ===

Classification Rules for Accounts Receivable:

1. Subject Line Keywords:
   a. If the subject contains any strong financial keywords such as “Bill,” “Billing,” “Lien,” “Payment,” “Balance,” “Records Request” (in a financial context), “W‑9,” or “Offer,” classify the email as Accounts Receivable.
   b. Even if imaging-related words are present (e.g., “IMG REQUEST”), if the subject also includes key billing indicators, classify the email as Accounts Receivable.

2. Content Patterns and Phrases:
   a. IF the content includes phrases like “bill balance,” “payment date,” “lien signed,” “process payment,” “generate bill,” “remittance,” “tax purposes,” “negotiation offer,” “e‑sign liens,” or explicit payment instructions, classify it as Accounts Receivable.
   b. In cases where imaging study details are mentioned, if they are accompanied or interwoven with billing information (e.g., “Bills Available,” “billing status,” financial balances), classify the email as Accounts Receivable.
   c. In ambiguous cases, determine the dominant theme based on the proportion of financial language relative to imaging details.

3. Associated Tags:
   a. Emails tagged with “Bill or balance Request,” “Bill Offer,” “Rad Lien,” “Tech Lien,” “ASL,” or “SLA Applies” are to be classified as Accounts Receivable, regardless of the presence of imaging details.
   b. Even if an email is tagged for imaging (e.g., “Report Request”), if billing or lien instructions are referenced, classify it as Accounts Receivable.

4. Purpose or Intent:
   a. Classify as Accounts Receivable if the email’s primary purpose is to discuss or request payment, provide billing information, negotiate payment terms, or process billing documents (e.g., liens, financial forms such as W‑9).
   b. IF the email involves legal/financial correspondence including patient or client payment instructions, or negotiation offers, the focus should clearly be billed under Accounts Receivable.

5. Unique Characteristics:
   a. Emails may combine patient identifiers with billing details (e.g., “Patient ID” with a bill balance or lien status). In such cases, if the financial information is prominent, classify as Accounts Receivable.
   b. Include emails that refer to billing portals, e-signing of liens, and negotiation of payment offers as strong indicators for Accounts Receivable, even if imaging terms are mentioned for context.

=== Medical Records & Images RULES ===

Classification Rules for Medical Records & Images:

1. Subject Line Keywords:
   a. If the subject contains one or more of the following imaging-specific keywords: “Imaging request,” “Images,” “Radiology film,” “MRI,” “CT,” “X‑ray,” “DICOM,” “CD Request” AND does NOT contain any strong billing/financial keywords (e.g., “bill,” “billing,” “payment,” “balance,” “lien,” “W‑9”), then consider it as a candidate for Medical Records & Images.
   b. If both imaging and billing keywords appear in the subject, review the content for the primary intent. IF the primary discussion is about accessing or transferring imaging data (e.g., links to view/download images or portals solely for imaging), then classify as Medical Records & Images; OTHERWISE, if financial processing is emphasized, defer to Accounts Receivable.

2. Content Keywords and Phrases:
   a. Classify as Medical Records & Images if the email contains phrases such as “images have been uploaded,” “view/download reports” (in an imaging context), “burn images onto a CD,” “push images into portal,” “radiology film request,” or mentions imaging modalities (MRI, CT, X‑ray, DICOM images) WITHOUT significant accompanying billing language.
   b. Exclude emails that include strong financial or billing terms (e.g., “bill balance,” “generate bill,” “lien,” “payment,” “negotiation offer,” “W‑9”) unless the overall context is clearly imaging data transfer.
      
3. Associated Tags:
   a. Apply the Medical Records & Images category if an email is tagged with “Image Request” or “Report Request” and there is no associated billing language.
   b. If tags indicate “Bill or balance Request,” “Bill Offer,” “Rad Lien,” “Tech Lien,” “ASL,” or “SLA Applies,” override imaging tags and classify the email under Accounts Receivable.

4. Purpose and Intent:
   a. IF the email’s main purpose is to request, share, or discuss medical imaging data (with no, or minimal, financing instructions), classify it as Medical Records & Images.
   b. IF imaging details are present solely as supplemental patient information and the email’s focus is on billing or lien processing, do not classify as Medical Records & Images.

5. Unique Characteristics:
   a. Look for exclusive instructions or links to imaging portals (that do not include billing/payment information).
   b. Disregard imaging references when accompanied by explicit financial instructions (e.g., e-sign liens, bill generation) which should follow the Accounts Receivable guidelines.
