Sub-Classification Rules for Scheduling Category
================================================
**Sub-Categories:** RX, Cancel Request, Reschedule Request, Scheduling request.

Please use the following rules to sub-classify the provided email content, already identified as 'Scheduling', into one of the above sub-categories.

--- START Scheduling Sub-Classification RULES ---

**PRIORITY RULE - ATTACHMENT-BASED CLASSIFICATION:**


1. **Primary Scheduling Intent**: Based on the email content, determine the primary intent related to scheduling and classify as follows:

   **CRITICAL DISTINCTION - Scheduling request vs RX:**

   **Scheduling request** = ACTION to book/schedule OR providing information to facilitate scheduling (future-focused)
   Examples: "Please schedule an appointment", "Book this patient for MRI", "Schedule imaging for this patient", "Contact patient and schedule", "Set up appointment", "When can we get the patient scheduled?", "Please advise when was the patient last called to schedule his appointment", "Here are the phone numbers for the patient" (when providing contact info for scheduling purposes)

   **RX** = STATUS inquiry about whether scheduling was completed OR details about an already-booked appointment OR prescription processing (checking confirmed appointments or orders)
   Examples: "Has the patient been scheduled?", "Were you able to schedule the client?", "Please confirm if client has been scheduled", "Did you schedule the appointment?", "What's the confirmed appointment date?", "Did you receive the referral?", "Is the order entered?", "Can you confirm the appointment time?", "What date is the patient coming in?", "Any update on my schedule request?", "Following up on scheduling"

   **Classification Rules:**

   - If the email contains an **active request to schedule, book, or set up an appointment** (using words like "please schedule", "book appointment", "set up imaging", "contact and schedule", "arrange appointment"), classify as 'Scheduling request'. This takes priority even if the email mentions prescriptions, referrals, or provides patient information for scheduling purposes.

   - If the email is **providing information that was requested to facilitate scheduling** (e.g., patient contact phone numbers, demographics, availability) in response to Precise Imaging's attempt to schedule or contact the patient, classify as 'Scheduling request'. Examples: "Here are the phone numbers for the patient", "Patient's correct contact info is...", "Patient is available on these dates".

   - If the email is a **status inquiry asking WHETHER an appointment was scheduled or completed** (e.g., "Has the patient been scheduled?", "Were you able to schedule the client?", "Did you schedule the appointment?", "Please confirm if client has been scheduled", "Any update on my schedule request?", "Following up on scheduling"), classify as 'RX'. This includes follow-ups on previous schedule requests asking if the scheduling action was completed.

   - If the email is a **status inquiry about details of an ALREADY-BOOKED/CONFIRMED appointment** (e.g., "What time is the patient's appointment?", "Can you confirm the appointment details?", "What date is already scheduled?", "Is the appointment still on?"), classify as 'RX'.

   - If the email concerns **prescription/referral processing, order entry status, or pharmacy coordination** WITHOUT actively requesting to schedule an appointment (e.g., "Order was entered", "Referral received", "Prescription being processed", "Has the referral been received?"), classify as 'RX'.

   - If the email clearly indicates that an **appointment is no longer happening or has been voided WITHOUT any mention of rescheduling**, classify as 'Cancel Request'.

   - **Cancel Request also includes:**
     - Canceling a **previously proposed reschedule** and keeping the original appointment time (e.g., "Please keep the appointment at the original time" after discussing moving it, "Never mind, keep it as scheduled", "Disregard the reschedule request")
     - Voiding or withdrawing a **proposed change** to an appointment
     - Any communication that **reverses a reschedule request** and maintains the current/original appointment
     - The key indicator: The appointment itself remains scheduled, but a proposed change is being canceled

   - If the email expresses a **request to change the date or time of an existing appointment**, classify as 'Reschedule Request'.

   - **NOTE**: If an email says "keep the appointment at [original time]" after a reschedule was discussed, this is a 'Cancel Request' (canceling the reschedule), NOT a 'Reschedule Request'.

   - If the email mentions both a **missed/no-show appointment AND an intent to reschedule** (e.g., "did not attend...reschedule", "no-show...need to reschedule", "missed appointment...call to reschedule"), classify as 'Reschedule Request'.

   **CRITICAL - RX Document Detection Rule:**
   - This rule applies ONLY if the "ATTACHED DOCUMENTS ANALYSIS" section above shows a document with `Document Type: rx`.
   - If the document analysis section shows a document with type "rx", classify as 'RX' regardless of email content.
   - **IMPORTANT**: Email text mentioning "attached Rx", "attached referral", or "see attached prescription" does NOT count as having an RX document. You MUST verify that the ATTACHED DOCUMENTS ANALYSIS section actually lists a document with type "rx".
   - If there is NO "ATTACHED DOCUMENTS ANALYSIS" section, or if the section does NOT contain any document with type "rx", then this rule does NOT apply - proceed with normal classification based on email content.

--- END Scheduling Sub-Classification RULES ---
